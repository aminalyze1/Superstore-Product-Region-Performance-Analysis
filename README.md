# 🛒 Superstore Product & Region Performance Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes a retail superstore dataset to evaluate **product and regional business performance** and identify opportunities for improving profitability and sales performance.

Using **Microsoft Excel**, I cleaned and transformed the dataset, performed exploratory and time-series analysis, developed business KPIs, and built interactive dashboards to turn raw sales records into actionable business insights.

The analysis focuses on two key areas:

* **Product performance** — identifying high- and low-performing categories and sub-categories.
* **Regional performance** — evaluating sales and profitability across states, cities, and regions.

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

Retail businesses need to understand not only how much they sell, but also **where revenue is being generated, which products are contributing to performance, and whether sales volume is translating into profit**.

This analysis was designed to answer:

> **Which products and regions are driving business performance, and where are there opportunities to improve profitability?**

### Key Business Questions

* Which product category generates the most sales?
* What are the top 10 best-selling products?
* How does profit performance vary across sub-categories?
* How does revenue change throughout the year?
* What is the average order value for sales and profit?
* Which states, cities, and regions perform best?
* Which regions generate high sales but relatively low profit?
* What KPIs can be used to monitor overall business health?

## 🔍 Key Insights

### Product Performance

* **Technology** emerged as the highest-performing product category.
* **Furniture** ranked lowest in overall performance.
* Within Furniture, **Tables** contributed significantly to the category's weaker performance despite stronger performance from other products.

### Sales Trend

Sales remained relatively steady through the middle months of the year before experiencing a significant increase from **August through December**.

Sales subsequently declined in January, indicating a notable seasonal pattern in demand.

### Regional Performance

* **Texas** recorded the highest sales among the states analyzed.
* Illinois ranked second, with a significant gap between the two.
* However, high sales volume did **not** translate into high profitability in Texas.
* Texas recorded the lowest profit performance, followed closely by Ohio.

This highlights an important business distinction:

> **High revenue does not necessarily mean high profitability.**

---

## 💡 Business Recommendations

### 1. Adopt a Profit-First Strategy in Underperforming States

Texas and Ohio require closer profitability monitoring despite their sales volumes.

Management should review discounting, pricing, operating costs, and other factors affecting margins before increasing sales investment.

### 2. Increase Focus on Technology

Technology demonstrated the strongest product-category performance.

The business could consider increasing marketing and sales investment in this category while continuing to monitor profitability and customer acquisition efficiency.

### 3. Review Furniture Operations

Furniture generates sales but does not consistently translate that volume into profit.

A deeper review of **product-level margins, pricing, discounts, costs, and delivery expenses** could help identify the sources of weak profitability.

### 4. Investigate Seasonal Demand

The August–December sales increase presents an opportunity to improve:

* Inventory planning
* Marketing campaigns
* Sales forecasting
* Resource allocation

Preparing for this period in advance could help the business capture seasonal demand more effectively.

## 🚧 Limitations & Next Steps

The analysis was primarily focused on **product and regional performance**.

Further analysis was limited by the available data.

Future analysis could include:

* **Basket analysis** to understand products frequently purchased together.
* **Customer analysis** to identify valuable customer segments and purchasing patterns.
* **Customer feedback analysis** to understand satisfaction and potential drivers of churn.
* More advanced profitability analysis to investigate the factors behind regional and product-level margin differences.

These additional datasets would allow the analysis to move beyond descriptive performance reporting toward deeper diagnostic and predictive insights.
